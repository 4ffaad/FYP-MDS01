## Required processing contract

1. **Pose front end.** The authors say public pose models tracked EMU patients poorly, so they manually annotated and fine-tuned a custom pose model; they recommend their `pose.pth` or fine-tuning on a similar scenario ([README, lines 11–18](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/README.md#L11-L18); paper, [§3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S2.SS1)). The linked [lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch](https://github.com/Daniil-Osokin/lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch) implementation uses 18 OpenPose keypoints in this order: nose, neck, right shoulder/elbow/wrist, left shoulder/elbow/wrist, right hip/knee/ankle, left hip/knee/ankle, right eye, left eye, right ear, left ear ([`modules/pose.py`, lines 8–16](https://github.com/Daniil-Osokin/lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch/blob/master/modules/pose.py#L8-L16)). Its model definition emits 19 heatmaps (18 keypoints plus background) and 38 PAF channels ([`models/with_mobilenet.py`, lines 89–123](https://github.com/Daniil-Osokin/lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch/blob/master/models/with_mobilenet.py#L89-L123)).
2. **18-to-15 convention.** The paper says VSViG excludes `l_ear`, `r_ear`, and `neck`, then uses five three-joint partitions: head (nose, left/right eye), right arm, right leg, left arm, and left leg ([§3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S2.SS3)). The model source hard-codes 15 points and five groups ([`VSViG.py`, lines 177–184 and 201–207](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L177-L207)). Preserve one explicit, tested mapping from the pose model's 18-keypoint order to those 15 positions.
3. **Patch extraction.** The paper describes a Gaussian-filtered RGB patch around each joint, with patch tensor \(P\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times T\times H\times W\times3}\) and embedding \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times T\times C}\) ([§3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S2.SS2)). The supplied implementation defaults to `kernel_size=128`, `kernel_sigma=0.3`, and `scale=1/4`; it pads the image, takes each keypoint-centered 128×128 crop, multiplies by the Gaussian kernel, rescales to 32×32, and returns 15 patches with 3 channels ([`extract_patches.py`, lines 6–28](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/extract_patches.py#L6-L28)). Its `norm` function maps each extracted crop to 0–255; the output array is created by `np.zeros` without an explicit dtype, and the file does not specify RGB/BGR conversion or a 0–1 scale. Coordinates are used as supplied (x at column 0, y at column 1); no coordinate normalization is implemented in these files.
4. **VSViG tensor shape.** The actual model `Stem.forward` unpacks `(B, T, P, C, H, W)` and the training comments give `(B, 30, 15, 3, 32, 32)` ([`VSViG.py`, lines 59–72](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L59-L72); [`train.py`, lines 17–29](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/train.py#L17-L29)). The paper's \(N\times T\times H\times W\times3\) notation omits batch and orders points before time; do not pass that mathematical order directly to the source implementation.
5. **Positional input is unresolved in upstream code.** The default constructors set `pos_emb='stem'` ([`VSViG.py`, lines 294–318](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L294-L318)). That path calls `Stem_pe`, which is constructed with `input_dim=3` and unpacks positional input as `(B,T,P,C)` ([`VSViG.py`, lines 74–87](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L74-L87); positional dispatch is at [lines 269–280](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L269-L280)). In contrast, `train.py` comments describe `kpts` as `(B,15,2)` and then apply an 18-index reorder before slicing to 15 ([`train.py`, lines 20–35](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/train.py#L20-L35)). Thus the repository does not provide a self-consistent, runnable statement of whether inference uses 2-D coordinates or a temporally repeated 3-feature tensor (for example, x/y plus confidence). Treat the positional feature count, time dimension, ordering, and normalization as **unreviewed** until confirmed against the checkpoint/training data.

The linked pose README documents the stock COCO pose model's input preprocessing as normalized planar BGR with mean `[128,128,128]` and scale `[1/256,1/256,1/256]` ([README, lines 93–96](https://github.com/Daniil-Osokin/lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch/blob/master/README.md#L93-L96)). That is a pose-front-end requirement, not proof of the VSViG patch tensor's color or scaling convention.

## Temporal facts and output semantics

- The paper's data setup uses 5-second clips at 30 FPS (150 source frames) and says fixed-stride sampling reduces them to 30 frames ([paper, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2)). The source also hard-codes `T = 30` ([`VSViG.py`, lines 201–207](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L201-L207)). The paper does **not** specify the exact sampled frame indices or offset, so 150→30 must not be silently turned into a claimed runtime `stride_frames=5` contract.
- For training-data construction, the paper says interictal clips are extracted without overlap, while transition and ictal clips use 4-second overlap ([paper, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2)). This is a dataset-sampling fact, not an implementation of an online inference window stride. The repository contains no video reader or window scheduler.
- In the model, the paper sets temporal kernel parameters `K_S=3`, `K_T=3`—one frame before and after in the temporal dimension—and the source uses a `(3,3,1)` 3-D convolution and stride `(2,1,1)` at stage transitions ([paper, §3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S2.SS5); [`VSViG.py`, lines 142–166](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L142-L166); [stage construction, lines 223–247](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L223-L247)).
- The forward pass returns one sigmoid value per batch item after global pooling ([`VSViG.py`, lines 282–292](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L282-L292)). The paper frames this as probability/likelihood regression rather than binary classification ([§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S2) and [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S3.SS1)). A sigmoid range is not evidence of calibration, clinical probability, or diagnosis.
- The authors' labels are 0 before EEG onset, 1 after clinical onset, and an increasing exponential value between them ([README, lines 25–30](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/README.md#L25-L30); paper, Appendix A). Those labels and onset definitions are not available automatically from an arbitrary video upload.

## Checkpoint and runtime dependencies

- `VSViG_base` and `VSViG_light` construct the networks but ignore the `pretrained` argument; the source calls `torch.load('PATH_TO_DYNAMIC_PARTITIONS')` rather than loading `VSViG-base.pth` ([`VSViG.py`, lines 294–320](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L294-L320)). A real adapter must supply the dynamic-order path and explicitly load the matching model state dict. The README separately tells users to add `dy_point_order.pt` and keep it unchanged for inference ([lines 32–34](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/README.md#L32-L34)).
- The model source needs PyTorch, torchvision, `timm`, and NumPy; patch extraction additionally needs OpenCV ([`VSViG.py`, lines 1–5](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/VSViG.py#L1-L5); [`extract_patches.py`, lines 1–4](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/extract_patches.py#L1-L4)). The linked pose repository adds `pycocotools`, OpenCV, NumPy, PyTorch, and torchvision ([`requirements.txt`](https://github.com/Daniil-Osokin/lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch/blob/master/requirements.txt)); its README documents the historical environment as Ubuntu 16.04, Python 3.6, and PyTorch 0.4.1 ([lines 29–38](https://github.com/Daniil-Osokin/lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch/blob/master/README.md#L29-L38)). No upstream file pins a compatible modern VSViG environment.
- `train.py` contains undefined placeholders `PATH_TO_DATA`, `PATH_TO_KPTS`, `PATH_TO_LABEL`, `PATH_TO_MODEL`, and `PATH_TO_DYNAMIC_PARTITIONS` ([lines 20–43](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/train.py#L20-L43)); it also compares lowercase model names against uppercase strings ([lines 46–60](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/train.py#L46-L60)). Therefore the repository is an implementation reference plus assets, not a drop-in inference/training package.

## Privacy, limitations, and licensing

- The VSViG README says the hospital required face masking; this prevents recognition of subtle facial changes early in seizure onset and may produce worse latency than the paper's reported result ([lines 20–23](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/README.md#L20-L23)). The paper describes EMU surveillance recordings as highly sensitive, says hospital data use was IRB-approved, and says release would follow removal of sensitive/private information ([paper, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S1) and [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S3.SS1)). Masking is a source-data measure, not a guarantee of anonymity for new deployments.
- The authors report a single-hospital dataset of 14 patients and 33 focal or generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and state future work is needed for more patients and seizure types ([paper, §4.1 and §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2)). Their reported error/latency/FDR numbers are results on that research dataset; they are not validation on this project's data.
- The paper says 32×32 patches and relative Gaussian `sigma=0.3` were suitable for 1920×1080 footage, but patch size depends on video resolution and patient size; too-small kernels lose RGB information and too-large kernels add contextual nuisance ([paper, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S3.SS2) and [§4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14775v2#S4.SS5)). Reusing those values on another camera requires a separate preprocessing review.
- Both the VSViG root [`LICENSE`](https://github.com/xuyankun/VSViG/blob/1026e7e7f2287b96f3cc375830f2836ffdf4588e/LICENSE) and the linked pose repository's root [`LICENSE`](https://github.com/Daniil-Osokin/lightweight-human-pose-estimation.pytorch/blob/master/LICENSE) are Apache License 2.0. The VSViG repository does not state separate terms for its checkpoints or the linked Hugging Face dataset in the files inspected; check asset and dataset terms before redistribution.

## Integration gate

Before calling an external VSViG bundle reviewed, freeze and document: the exact VSViG and lightweight-OpenPose revisions; checkpoint/state-dict compatibility; the 18-to-15 keypoint mapping; single-person selection behavior; positional feature count and time replication; coordinate scale; color/channel order; patch normalization; sampled frame indices; clip/window stride; output score/threshold semantics; and privacy/retention handling. MDS01 records one executable adapter choice for these fields, but the unresolved upstream items remain reproduction risks. Until those facts are confirmed against the original training artifacts, treat the result as an explicitly approved research integration rather than a faithful reproduction. Do not describe its sigmoid output as calibrated or clinically validated, and do not claim performance transfer to this project's data.
